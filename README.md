1.	Project Background

Business communities in the United States are experiencing a high demand for human resources, and one of their ongoing challenges is finding and attracting the right talent, which is crucial for maintaining competitiveness. Companies in the US seek hardworking, skilled, and qualified individuals both domestically and internationally.

The Immigration and Nationality Act (INA) allows foreign workers to enter the United States for temporary or permanent employment. This act also protects US workers from any negative impacts on their wages or working conditions by requiring employers to adhere to statutory requirements when hiring foreign workers to address labour shortages. The Office of Foreign Labor Certification (OFLC) manages these immigration programs.

OFLC reviews job certification applications from employers who wish to bring foreign workers to the US. It grants certifications if employers can prove there are not enough US workers available to perform the job at wages that meet or exceed the standard for the occupation in the intended employment area.
 
2.	Objective

In FY 2016, the Office of Foreign Labor Certification (OFLC) processed 775,979 employer applications for 1,699,957 positions requiring temporary or permanent labour certifications. This represented a 9% increase in the number of applications processed compared to the previous year. As the volume of applications grows annually, reviewing each case is becoming increasingly labour-intensive.
To address this challenge, there is a need for a Machine Learning-based solution to assist in shortlisting candidates with a higher likelihood of visa approval. The OFLC has engaged EasyVisa to provide data-driven solutions. 

Our primary focus is to analyse the data using classification model to:

* Streamline the visa approval process.
* Recommend whether to certify or deny visas for applicants, based on key factors that significantly impact the case status.

3.	Actionable Insights & Recommendations

* A predictive model has been developed to assess employees who are likely to be approved for visas. By identifying the key factors driving the visa approval process, the OFLC can take appropriate actions to select the right candidates for employment in the U.S.
* Factors that drive visa approval process includes education of employee (High school), has the employee have a job experience and prevailing wages provided by the company.
* Employees with only a high school education are typically denied visa approval, as a bachelor's degree is generally required for jobs in the U.S. Most companies necessitate at least a graduate-level education. While a master’s or doctorate is not mandatory, having a basic level of education in the relevant field is essential.
* Nearly 65% of employees with prior job experience are granted visas, indicating that having previous work experience is often essential for companies when offering job opportunities. The time and resources invested in training new hires are significant considerations for companies. Therefore, it is crucial to evaluate an immigrant's prior experience when considering their visa approval process.
* Prevailing wage is also a crucial factor in visa approval, as the investment in an employee's compensation is a key consideration for hiring. Ensuring that the offered wage meets or exceeds industry standards is essential for securing approval.
* The number of employees, the year a company was established, or whether a job is full-time are not significant factors in the visa approval or denial process.
